# About the Author {.unnumbered}

```{=latex}
\markboth{ABOUT THE AUTHOR}{ABOUT THE AUTHOR}
```


David Silva is a software engineer with over fifteen years building production
systems. His work has spanned public-sector services at GOV.UK and the UK
Ministry of Justice, fintech platforms at Creditspring and Tembo Money, and
large-scale recruitment infrastructure at Indeed.

Vanilla Roguelike, the codebase this book is built on, took five years to
reach the form it has today. It started as a few hundred lines of Ruby
exploring maze algorithms and grew, through several breaking points, into a
complete game with proper Entity-Component-System architecture, event-driven
logging, and a test suite you can actually trust. The chapters here follow
that journey honestly: the architecture problem in Chapter 8 was a real
breaking point in the codebase, the move to ECS was a real refactor, and the
testing patterns in Chapter 19 are the ones that held up under change. He
wrote this book because he could not find one that explained those lessons in
working Ruby code, with the trade-offs on the page rather than hidden behind
framework conventions.

His other book, *Modular Rails: Architecture for the Long Game*, applies the
same thinking to large Rails monoliths — using mountable engines to decompose
them without reaching for microservices. Where this book argues for clean
architecture in a small Ruby codebase, *Modular Rails* argues for it in a
large one.
